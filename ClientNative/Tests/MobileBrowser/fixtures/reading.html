A quiet morning by the sea

The harbor was still quiet when we reached the waterfront. A few fishing boats moved past the breakwater, and the first light was beginning to reach the roofs along the shore.

We followed the narrow path beside the sea. The tide had left a line of small shells on the sand, and there was enough wind to carry the sound of the water all the way to the road.

A bakery near the station had just opened. We bought bread and coffee, then found a bench facing the boats. For a while, nobody had anywhere else to be.

By midmorning the market was waking up. Shopkeepers rolled back their shutters, stacked empty crates beside the doors, and greeted the neighbors walking to the station.

Past the last row of houses, the path climbed above the coast. Each turn revealed another stretch of water. We stopped often, sometimes to take a photograph and sometimes simply to look.

The walk back took longer than we expected. A small bookshop had a table outside, and a cafe was serving lunch through an open window. We put the map away and followed the streets toward the harbor.

When we finally reached the station, the morning boats were returning. The platforms were busier now, but the sea beyond the houses looked much as it had when we arrived.

There was time for one last walk along the waterfront before the train. We kept to the shaded side of the street and stopped at the same bench, where the tide had already changed.
